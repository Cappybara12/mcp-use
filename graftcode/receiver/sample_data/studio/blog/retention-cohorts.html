How to Read a Retention Cohort Table in 5 Minutes

What does a retention table actually tell you? Less than you think, and more than you fear.

Rows are groups, columns are time

Each row is a group of users who joined in the same week. Each column is how many came back later. Read across a row to see one group age.

Look for the flattening

Healthy products flatten after a few weeks. If a row keeps falling, users never found the reason to stay.

Compare rows, not cells

One bad cell is noise. A whole column that falls week after week is a signal.

Act on one thing

Pick the earliest week where the curve bends and study what those users did differently. Read the Amplitude guide to retention for more cases.

  • Write the goal down.
  • Pick one measure.
  • Review it weekly.

Keep it simple. A small plan you follow beats a perfect plan you skip, so begin with one change this week.

A worked example

Imagine a group of 500 users who joined in March. After one week 62% return. After four weeks 38% return. After twelve weeks 31% return. The drop from week one to four is steep, but the line then flattens. That flat line is the group that found value. Your job is to learn what those 31% did in their first week. Look at the first three actions they took. Then check whether the users who left ever took them. Often the gap is one missing step.

What to do this week

Open your newest cohort and your oldest cohort side by side. Mark the week where each curve bends. If the new group bends later or lower, something changed in onboarding. Write down what shipped in that period. Ask support what users said. Pick one fix and measure the next cohort. Keep the table in one shared place, so every review starts from the same numbers.

FAQ

What is a cohort?

A group of users who share a start date or behaviour.

What is a good retention rate?

It depends on the product. Compare with your own history first.

Why does the curve flatten?

Because the people who stay have found real value.
