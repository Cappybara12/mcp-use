5 Pricing Page Tests Worth Running This Quarter

Is your pricing page doing its job? Only a test can tell you, and most pages have never had one.

Test the headline first

Change one line and measure signups for two weeks. Small copy changes often move results by 10% or more.

Show the annual price

Many buyers compare monthly numbers. Showing the yearly saving in plain words can lift upgrades.

Cut one plan

Three plans beat five. Fewer choices speed up decisions.

Add proof near the button

A short customer quote next to the call to action answers the last doubt. Read the pricing research hub for more ideas.

Keep it simple. A small plan you follow beats a perfect plan you skip, so begin with one change this week.

A worked example

One team changed the headline from a feature list to a plain promise. Signups moved from 3.1% to 3.6% over two weeks. A second team showed the yearly saving as a number of months free. Upgrades rose by 9%. Neither team changed the product or the price. They only changed how the page spoke to a worried buyer. Both kept a log of the test, the dates, and the result. That log became their playbook for the next quarter, and it stopped arguments about opinions.

What to do this week

Choose one test and write down your guess before you start. Set the end date at two weeks. Do not peek at the result every day. When the test ends, record the numbers and what you learned. If the result is flat, make the next change bigger. If it wins, keep it and move to the next idea on the list. Share the result with sales, who hear the same objections.

FAQ

How long should a test run?

Two full weeks, so every weekday is covered.

What if results are flat?

Keep the original and test a bigger change next.
