The Onboarding Checklist We Give Every New Customer

Where do new users get stuck in the first week? We checked 200 accounts, and the answer was boring.

Day one: one clear task

Ask for one action only. Connect a data source. Everything else can wait.

Day three: show a result

Send a short note with the first chart built from their own data. People return when they see themselves in the product.

Day seven: invite a teammate

Accounts with two or more users are far more likely to stay. Make the invite easy.

Keep the list short

Five items beat twenty. Look at how Intercom frames onboarding for another view.

  • Write the goal down.
  • Pick one measure.
  • Review it weekly.

Keep it simple. A small plan you follow beats a perfect plan you skip, so begin with one change this week.

A worked example

One customer connected a data source on day one but never invited anyone. Another invited two teammates by day four. Six weeks later the first account was gone and the second was expanding. The pattern held across 200 accounts. People stay when the product becomes part of how a team works. So our checklist pushes every new account toward one shared result in the first week. It does not teach every feature. It teaches the one habit that matters.

What to do this week

Write down the single action that predicts retention in your product. Make it the first item on your checklist. Remove anything that does not help people reach it. Send one short message on day one, day three and day seven. Read the replies yourself. If many people stall at the same step, fix that step before adding anything new.

FAQ

How long should onboarding take?

The first useful result should arrive in under ten minutes.

Should onboarding be automated?

Automate the steps, but keep one real person available.
