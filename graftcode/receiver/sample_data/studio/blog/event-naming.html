A Simple Naming Guide for Analytics Events

Why do some teams trust their data and others do not? Often it comes down to how events are named.

Use one pattern

Pick object then action, such as project_created. Write it down and never break it.

Keep names short

Short names are easier to type, search and remember. Move detail into properties.

Review before shipping

One person owns the tracking plan. Every new event gets a quick review. This takes five minutes and saves days of cleanup.

Retire old events

Delete events nobody uses. A smaller plan is a plan people follow. The Segment naming guide has good examples.

  • Write the goal down.
  • Pick one measure.
  • Review it weekly.

Keep it simple. A small plan you follow beats a perfect plan you skip, so begin with one change this week.

A worked example

Compare two plans. The first has events like click, button_click and Clicked Button. Nobody knows which one to trust. The second has project_created, report_shared and invite_sent. Anyone can read it and understand the product. The second plan took one hour to write. The first took two years to untangle. When a new teammate joins, they can find any event in seconds. That alone is worth the effort. It also makes dashboards cheaper to build and easier to check.

What to do this week

List your ten most important events. Rename any that break the pattern. Add one sentence that explains each event and who owns it. Put the list where everyone can find it. Then agree on one simple rule: no new event ships without an entry in the plan. Review the list once a quarter and retire what nobody uses.

FAQ

Should event names be lowercase?

Yes, with underscores. It avoids duplicates.

How many events is too many?

If nobody can list them, you have too many.

Start by listing your ten most important events today, then compare them with this pattern.
